### Why Does Traditional Custom Tailoring Require So Many Fittings?

You have likely heard your peers share their tailoring experiences: weeks spent trying on half-finished baste garments, with the entire process dragging on for months. This is not due to a lack of artisan skill, but rather an inherent limitation of the legacy system that prevents it from being both elegant and efficient.

Traditional haute couture relies entirely on manual labor. During the initial consultation, a tailor records measurements with a physical tape. A rough muslin or canvas prototype is then constructed. In the second meeting, you don\'t wear a finished piece; you stand still while pins are repeatedly marked across your silhouette. The prototype is deconstructed, altered, and you are called back for a third confirmation. If your posture features unique asymmetries, the fitting loop can expand indefinitely.

Why can\'t it be done right the first time? Because manual measurement only captures flat, static dimensions. It cannot predict how luxury fabrics distribute dynamic tension when your body moves. Baste fittings are essentially physical trial-and-error. The time cost is naturally exorbitant. This is precisely why **made to measure suits** and **luxury custom tailoring** are historically synonymous with agonizing wait times.
