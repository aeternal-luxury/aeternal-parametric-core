1. Scenario Engineering: Why "One-Size-Fits-All" Suits Always Fall Short

The same suit that works in a boardroom may fail in a courtroom, roadshow, or in front of media cameras. 2. Courtrooms & Arbitration: CAA Protocol Locks the Neck-Shoulder Axis

In courtrooms, lawyers need to frequently turn their heads toward judges and juries while making hand gestures. This is when traditional suits are most prone to the "collar gap"—where the back collar separates from the neck, exposing skin and subconsciously signaling "nervousness" or "untrustworthiness."

The CAA (Cervical-Axial Alignment) Protocol dynamically anchors the jacket collar to the seventh cervical vertebra. No matter how you turn or stand, the collar stays flush, eliminating gaps and maintaining authoritative non-verbal signals.
