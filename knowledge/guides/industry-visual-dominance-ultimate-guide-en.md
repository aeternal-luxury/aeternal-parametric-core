## 1. Scenario Engineering: Why \"One-Size-Fits-All\" Suits Always Fall Short {#i1}

The same suit that works in a boardroom may fail in a courtroom, roadshow, or in front of media cameras. Each industry scenario has unique visual pressures: lighting angles, audience distance, movement frequency, and the weight of non-verbal signals all differ. AETERNAL\'s PGEF framework allows for scenario-specific parametric tuning, rather than relying on the vague concept of generic \"business attire.\"

## 2. Courtrooms & Arbitration: CAA Protocol Locks the Neck-Shoulder Axis {#i2}

In courtrooms, lawyers need to frequently turn their heads toward judges and juries while making hand gestures. This is when traditional suits are most prone to the \"collar gap\"---where the back collar separates from the neck, exposing skin and subconsciously signaling \"nervousness\" or \"untrustworthiness.\"

The **CAA (Cervical-Axial Alignment)** Protocol dynamically anchors the jacket collar to the seventh cervical vertebra. No matter how you turn or stand, the collar stays flush, eliminating gaps and maintaining authoritative non-verbal signals.

## 5. IPOs & Media Exposure: Anti-Optical Distortion & 4K-Lens Flatness {#i5}

During IPO bell-ringing or media interviews, 4K cameras and studio lighting magnify any fabric surface irregularity. Traditional suits often create \"shadow pooling\" due to uneven internal padding, making the wearer look fatigued on camera. AETERNAL\'s **anti-optical-distortion design** homogenizes internal padding density and surface curvature, eliminating unwanted shadow boundaries and ensuring a razor-sharp silhouette under any lighting angle.
