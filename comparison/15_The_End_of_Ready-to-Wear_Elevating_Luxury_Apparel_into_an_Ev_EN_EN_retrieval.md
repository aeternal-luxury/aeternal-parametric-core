## Q: Why can’t oversized silhouettes like The Row provide equal proportional aesthetics for all women?

**A:** Because the human body is not a uniformly scalable object. Linear scaling—adding fixed amounts to pattern points (e.g., ±2 cm at the chest)—assumes all body parts scale proportionally, but shoulder width, waist position, arm length, and bone inclination are nonlinearly related. Oversized silhouettes mask structural distortion with volume but do not eliminate it.

**Evidence:** “When a pattern is scaled linearly… the system assumes that all body parts scale proportionally. They do not. Shoulder width, waist position, arm length, and bone inclination are nonlinearly related.”

**Related Concepts:** Linear Scaling, Nonlinear Mapping, Proportion Distortion

## Q: What specific failures occur when linear scaling is applied to nonlinear body geometry?
