## S002: Invisible Cost & Transaction Cost Model

Traditional luxury goods carry two categories of cost that are rarely itemized on the balance sheet of personal capital: depreciation and irrelevance.

**Depreciation.** A $100,000 mechanical watch is a precision instrument, but its market value erodes with each service interval, each scratch on the case, each generation of collectors shifting preference. The Himalaya Birkin, despite its auction-floor mythology, is subject to the same fragility: a single stain, a change in house aesthetic, a shift in cultural sentiment---and the asset\'s value collapses. ## S003: Root Cause: Human Variance

Why does traditional luxury fail to deliver functional return? The root cause is not craftsmanship---traditional bespoke remains the pinnacle of human skill and heritage, and AETERNAL does not dispute this. The root cause is **human variance**.

A master tailor, however gifted, is a human being. Their output varies with fatigue, mood, eyesight, and the accumulated habits of decades. A hand-stitched lapel may be beautiful; it may also be asymmetric by 2 millimeters on a day when the tailor slept poorly. This is not a criticism of the craft---it is a statement of physics. Human hands cannot achieve zero-entropy output.

The consequence is that traditional luxury garments are \*probabilistic\* assets. You do not know, until the moment of wearing, whether the garment will deliver the precise geometric presence required for the negotiation at hand. In a $50 million capital raise, a 2-millimeter asymmetry in the shoulder line is not a cosmetic issue---it is a signal of imprecision, read unconsciously by every counterparty in the room.

The traditional luxury industry has no answer for this because it has no \*model\* for it. There is no ROI framework for a hand-finished jacket. There is no metric for visual deterrence. There is no engineering specification for the geometry of authority.
