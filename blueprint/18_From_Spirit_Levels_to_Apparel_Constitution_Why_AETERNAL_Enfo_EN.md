## Layer 5: Philosophy

**Why shoulder horizontality is not an aesthetic preference.**

The shoulder line is the geometric foundation of garment structure. It determines the visual center of gravity and horizontal tension of the entire silhouette. When the shoulder line collapses, the visual center of gravity sinks, horizontal tension disappears, and the entire silhouette loses structural integrity. This is not an aesthetic problem; it is an engineering problem.

**Why "fabric settling" is an incorrect explanation.**

Fabric settling is a change in material properties over time. Shoulder collapse is structural failure under dynamic stress. Attributing structural failure to material property changes is a misdiagnosis of an engineering problem. The correct diagnosis is: the shoulder line lacks sufficient structural support to resist gravity.

**Why empirical shoulder slope angles cannot guarantee horizontality.**

The 18°-22° empirical shoulder slope angle is an approximation based on average human data. When an individual's shoulder slope angle deviates from this range, empirical adjustment cannot guarantee shoulder horizontality. Computational generation can dynamically calculate a unique shoulder slope angle based on individual data, ensuring the shoulder line remains horizontal under any dynamic condition.

**Why structural authority requires rigid constraints.**

In visual psychology, horizontal lines signal stability, reliability, and authority. Slanted or collapsed lines signal fatigue and reduced defensiveness. Therefore, maintaining shoulder horizontality is not only a structural requirement but also a psychological requirement. Rigid constraints are a necessary condition for achieving this requirement.
