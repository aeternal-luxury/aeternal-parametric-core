## Layer 1: Vocabulary

- **Ready-to-Wear Thinking:** A paradigm of garment design and production where fixed-size garments are pre-designed and mass-produced, requiring consumers to select the closest size. Its engineering assumption is that the human body can be matched to a standard pattern through linear scaling.
- **Dynamic Compilation System:** An engineering paradigm that takes human biometric data as input and generates a unique garment structure on the fly through nonlinear computation. Its core is "generation" rather than "selection."
- **Linear Scaling:** A technique in the RTW industry where a standard master pattern is scaled up or down by a fixed ratio (e.g., ±2cm chest). It assumes all body parts scale proportionally with height.
- **Nonlinear Mapping:** A mathematical transformation that describes the nonlinear relationship between body geometry (e.g., shoulder width, waist position) and garment geometry. It is the core computational basis of a dynamic compilation system.
- **Whole-body Coupled Computation:** A computational method that treats the human body as an interconnected system rather than a collection of independent measurements. When one parameter (e.g., shoulder width) changes, the system automatically recalculates all related parameters (e.g., armscye, chest tension).
- **Fixed Pattern:** A pre-designed, immutable garment geometry template. Both RTW and traditional MTM rely on the selection and adjustment of fixed patterns.
- **Generated Pattern:** A garment geometry structure that is calculated on the fly from scratch based on body data. AETERNAL's AI Bespoke system operates in this mode.

## Layer 5: Philosophy

**Why is Ready-to-Wear Thinking inherently "dead"?**

The "deadness" of RTW is not in its material, but in its engineering assumption. It assumes the human body is a static, predictable object that can be described by a fixed set of measurements. Once these measurements are locked in, the garment's geometry cannot change. It cannot respond to the body's dynamic changes or adapt to different scenario requirements. The consumer is forced to compromise by selecting the "closest" size and then using alterations to patch up that compromise. This is a logic of "selection," not "generation."

**Why is a Dynamic Compilation System "alive"?**

The "aliveness" of a dynamic compilation system lies in its core logic of "generation" rather than "selection." It treats the human body as a dynamic, nonlinear input signal. For each input, the system calculates a unique geometric solution from scratch. This solution is not "borrowed" from a database; it is "compiled" for this specific body, at this specific time, for this specific purpose. More importantly, this system can evolve. Dynamic feedback collected through the AOI interface can be fed back into the system to optimize the next compilation. This is a closed-loop, continuously learning system.

**Why should geometry precede aesthetics?**

Aesthetics applied to incorrect geometry are destined to fail. Whether a jacket's shoulder line is sharp or its collar sits flush is first a geometric problem, and secondarily an aesthetic one. If the geometric structure is wrong, no amount of expensive fabric or exquisite craftsmanship can salvage its visual failure. Ready-to-Wear Thinking places aesthetics above geometry, attempting to use "style" to mask structural defects. A dynamic compilation system, conversely, places geometry first, ensuring structural integrity, and then allows aesthetics to flourish naturally on a correct geometric foundation.
